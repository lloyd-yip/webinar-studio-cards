Implications
  1. Show is still the emergency. Show was 7.6% this week, after 9.8% in W159 and 6.4% in W160, so this is the third red week in a row. Studio puts the net-new show rate at about 8.2%, which is far below the 14.5% net-new show rate in W159. Keep the reminder and join-flow fixes moving, and get the second-calendar-invite test to net-new registrants live before W162 on Oct 20.
  2. Books came back once the webinar was on WebinarGeek again. 13 people booked, which is a 10.8% Book % against the people who stayed ten minutes. The two Zoom weeks ran at 6.1% and 5.2%, and W161 is back inside the 8.3% to 15.2% range of the earlier WebinarGeek weeks. This supports the Sep 30 finding that the Zoom tape never told people where to book, so any new webinar tool must carry a clear booking call-to-action before it goes live. Books usually keep rising for a few days, so this number will move.
  3. More invites bought fewer regs. This week had the most invites in the set, but regs fell to 2,277. That makes the reg rate 0.86%, the lowest of all ten weeks shown here. It fits the worry that the team leans too hard on one big audience list, so the invite copy test and the recycled-leads batch matter more this week.
  4. Nonjoiners are still more than half the list. 1,271 of the 2,277 regs were people re-invited after missing an earlier webinar, which is about 56% and the third week in a row above half. Only 1,006 regs were net-new, the lowest net-new count in this set. Adding fresh and recycled leads is the way to lift the net-new share.
  5. Fewer of the people who joined stayed. About 69% of the live room stayed ten minutes or more, down from about 80% in W160. That is still a decent hold, but watch it next week so a smaller room does not also start leaving earlier.
