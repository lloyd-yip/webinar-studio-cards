Implications
  1. Same story two weeks running. WebinarJam showed 23.1% of its regs again, more than three times Demio at 6.7%. But Demio turned 1.70% of its invites into regs, while WebinarJam only turned 0.45% into regs. So the two halves landed almost the same live room, 155 on Demio and 134 on WebinarJam, from about the same invite count. Two matching weeks make this a real pattern, not noise, so it’s time to dig into why WebinarJam loses so many people at sign-up.
  2. WebinarJam is now winning on books too. WebinarJam booked 7 people from 98 who stayed ten minutes, which is a 7.1% Book %. Demio booked 5 from 110, which is 4.5%. That flips last week’s early read, when Demio was slightly ahead, so WebinarJam now leads on show and on booking. Both are still well below W161’s 10.8% on WebinarGeek, so keep checking that both tapes and chats tell people exactly where to book. Books keep rising for a few days, so these numbers will move.
  3. This week was meant to run on one tool. The plan from the Oct 19 sprint was to run this week on a single tool while Lloyd is off-road, but W163 ran on Demio and WebinarJam again. That’s fine for the comparison, since it gives a clean second week of data. Someone should confirm whether running both was a deliberate call, so next week’s setup isn’t a surprise.
  4. Demio still leans hard on nonjoiners. 1,373 of Demio’s 2,319 regs were people re-invited after missing an earlier webinar, which is about 59%, down from 68% last week. That leaves 946 net-new Demio regs. The WebinarJam split still isn’t built in Studio, so the full-week mix is unknown, and generating that report would close the gap on both W162 and W163.
  5. The card landed a day late again. The invite ledger and Demio scorecard only landed Wednesday morning, and the deep sync finished at about 9:01am ET. If this keeps happening, the Tuesday card will always be thin, so it’s worth asking whether the Studio report and deep sync can run Tuesday afternoon instead.
